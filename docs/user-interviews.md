# User interviews — how creators select footage

How videographers and editors find the good parts of their footage today.
These notes guide the design of the SELECTS interface.

People are referred to as P1, P2, P3… Real names are never written here.

---

## Lucas — project author

```
Participant: Lucas
Date: 2026-11-18
Type of videos they make: Monthly content for different businesses, weddings
  and a mix of different events.
Cameras and editing software: Sony FX3 and, for editing, DaVinci Resolve.
Footage filmed for one finished video: Depends on the event, but about
  40–50 min for a 3 min video.

How they select footage today (last real project, step by step): Copies the
  cards to a drive, sorts the clips into a folder per client or event, imports
  them into Resolve and watches every clip, marking the usable parts. For
  weddings and events, also searches long recordings for key moments such as
  vows, speeches and reactions.
Time spent selecting, compared with the rest of the edit: The longest part of
  the edit, especially for weddings and events with a lot of footage.
Tools used for selecting (bins, markers, flags, ratings, folders, notes):
  Folders per client or event, Resolve bins and markers.

How they choose between several takes of the same line: Watches all the takes
  of the line and compares them to pick the right one. Would like a tool to do
  the first pass.
How they decide where a usable B-roll segment starts and ends: Cuts away the
  start and end of a clip, where the camera is still moving, and any part in
  the middle where the camera moves unnaturally.
What makes them throw a clip away immediately: Missed focus, shaky footage,
  wrong exposure and accidental recordings.

Biggest frustration when selecting: Watching 40–50 minutes of footage to find
  the few minutes that make a 3 minute video, and repeating it every month for
  each client.
A good shot they lost or could not find later: A good moment buried in a long
  clip that was only noticed after the edit was almost finished.

What a tool must show for them to trust a suggested take: It should mark the
  best take, keep the other takes available to choose from, and give the reason
  for its choice.
What would make them ignore the suggestion: If it picks blurry or shaky shots,
  or decides alone without showing the other takes.

Surprising things they said: Prefers a tool that explains its choice rather
  than one that decides alone. The final decision stays with the editor.
```

```
Participant: proto-persona "Andrei"
Date: 2026-11-19
Type of videos they make: Short brand videos and personal travel films.
  Mix of talking-to-camera clips (ads, vlogs) and B-roll (landscapes, city,
  details). Some videos are vertical for Instagram and TikTok.
Cameras and editing software: Sony FX30 in S-Log3 (XAVC S 4K, 25p) and a phone
  for quick shots. Edits and grades in DaVinci Resolve.
Footage filmed for one finished video: 40–90 minutes of footage for a
  1–3 minute video.

How they select footage today: Copies the card to an external SSD, renames
  the clips, imports everything into one Resolve bin, then scrubs through every
  clip and marks good parts with markers. Creates a "Selects" timeline by hand.
Time spent selecting, compared with the rest of the edit: About a third of
  the whole edit, often 2–3 hours per project. The most boring part.
Tools used for selecting: Resolve bins, colored markers, clip colors,
  sometimes a "selects" timeline. No written notes.

How they choose between several takes of the same line: Watches every take,
  looks for the one with no mistakes, no hesitation and good focus. Often
  forgets which take was best and has to watch them again.
How they decide where a usable B-roll segment starts and ends: Skips the first
  and last seconds where the camera moves when starting or stopping the
  recording, and any moment where the camera shakes or the gimbal corrects.
What makes them throw a clip away immediately: Missed focus, accidental
  recordings (the ground, a pocket), wrong exposure, someone walking into frame.

Biggest frustration when selecting: Watching hours of footage to find a few
  seconds, and doing it again when coming back to a project weeks later.
A good shot they lost or could not find later: A sunset shot that was buried
  in a long clip and only found after the video was already published.

What a tool must show for them to trust a suggested take: The reason for the
  choice (e.g. "stable camera, sharp focus, no hesitation"), the exact time
  range, and quick access to the other takes to compare.
What would make them ignore the suggestion: If it picks blurry or shaky shots,
  or if it hides the other takes and decides alone.

Surprising things they said: Prefers a tool that explains its choice over one
  that is always right but silent. The final decision must stay with the editor.
```
